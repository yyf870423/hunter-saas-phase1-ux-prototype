Client research:
decide if it’s worth pursuing, then how to open

Client research is a capability that weighs funding, open roles, the founding team, business changes and public risks together. It answers three questions: is this client worth pursuing, is now the right time, and can you deliver.

Funding news arrives every day, but very few clients actually open searches to headhunters. HunterBuddy helps you tell “looks doable” from “really doable”.

Boseek·Updated 2026-11-21

01

Why months of pursuit end with nothing

  • Plenty of opportunities in new sectors, no clear way in

    Every new direction means gathering a lot of material and learning the value chain. Cast too wide, and you turn up a pile of companies that only “look relevant”, with no idea where to start.

  • Plenty of funding news, little real need

    Funding doesn’t mean hiring right away, and hiring doesn’t mean opening a search to headhunters. Analyzing each one takes time, and the answer may just be “no need for now”.

  • The search looks doable, but the delivery and payment risks aren’t clear

    Where the candidates are, whether they can be reached, whether the client can pay and whether the role will last all decide whether to take it on. A start-up’s option pool, nominee holdings and buy-back terms often can’t be confirmed from public information alone.

  • A hot sector isn’t the same as a good client

    A star team and an impressive round don’t automatically make a good client. Whether the core technology is real, whether the founding team is stable and whether there are compliance risks: miss any of these and the effort may be wasted.

  • A vague client, and no clear next step

    “Send us something to look at” or “we already work with a headhunter” is neither a no nor an opening. Who decides, and who has the standing to introduce you, is often unclear.

  • An existing client grows a new business, and nobody follows it up

    When an existing client sets up a new department, enters a new sector or changes leaders, it is usually the first client worth developing. Busy with delivery, teams let these chances slip past.

02

How HunterBuddy researches a client

  1. Build an opportunity map first, not a list of buzzwords

    Around the target industry and technical direction, it lays out the relevant companies, funding news, business positioning, founding teams and open roles, narrowing things down to those worth a look.

  2. Check the real need against objective signals

    Open roles are the core evidence of need. It checks funding announcements, careers pages, new businesses, team changes and public tenders against each other. Only verifiable signals, such as hiring after a round, roles posted steadily or a new department, move a company into priority follow-up.

  3. Clear the risks first, then look at funding and team

    It checks the funding stage and the background of the founders and core team. If there are clear problems with money, compliance or the core team, it flags them and goes no further.

  4. Judge the need, the ability to pay and whether you can deliver

    Taking the funding round, company stage, open roles, business size and past signals together, it judges whether the client really needs a headhunter, can afford one, and whether the role will last.

  5. Find the decision chain and the way in

    Clients at different stages call for different people. Drawing on organizational relationships, public titles and your existing network, it suggests the likely decision makers, routes to an introduction and reasons to get in touch.

  6. Keep delivering for existing clients, and follow their new lines of business

    When an existing client gets a new department, a new family of roles or a new leader, it points out the likely new need and marks the delivery challenges.

Where it stops

HunterBuddy doesn’t contact clients for you, and doesn’t treat funding news as a need. It lays out the evidence, the risks, the routes to an introduction and the next step.
