Identity checks:
from one ID to one real person

Identity checks are a capability that works back from an alias, an English name, a community handle or a platform username to a person’s real name and identity. They rule out namesakes by cross-checking several sources, and never guess Chinese characters from pinyin.

Senior technical people often appear under an English name, an alias or a GitHub ID. Without the real name, none of the papers, patents, career history or colleague connections that follow can be linked up. HunterBuddy turns this step into reasoning you can check, not a single guess.

Boseek·Updated 2026-11-21

01

Why one name is so hard to confirm

  • One person, three or four identities

    A Maimai nickname, a GitHub ID, an English name, a Chinese name and platform usernames all differ. The material is spread across platforms, and duplicate records keep piling up.

  • Pinyin fits many characters, and one wrong guess finds the wrong person

    “Li Wei” could be any of several Chinese names. Guessing characters from pinyin has an error rate no one can control, and a wrong guess raises no alarm.

  • Too many namesakes, and a shared name isn’t a shared person

    One Chinese name may belong to two engineers at one company and a researcher at another. Merge on name alone, and two people become one profile.

  • The person behind the alias is often the one you want

    The more senior the engineer, the more likely they go by an English name or a community ID. These people have the thinnest public records, and they’re exactly the ones most worth finding.

  • Checking platform by platform is costly and never reused

    Confirming one person can mean reading papers, searching patents, looking at GitHub commits and comparing education. Once it’s done, the next person means starting over.

  • Getting the wrong person costs more than not finding them

    Reaching out to the wrong person costs more than time. It costs your standing in a small professional circle.

02

How HunterBuddy works out who someone is

  1. Anchors first, the name second

    It compares anchors such as education, work history, timeline, technical direction and collaborators to judge whether several records belong to the same person. The more anchors, the firmer the conclusion.

  2. The name is the result, the evidence is the basis

    Every identity finding names its sources: which paper, which stint, which public talk. You can click through to the original material.

  3. Namesakes ruled out before anything is confirmed

    It compares organizations, dates, research directions and collaboration networks to tell different people with the same name apart. Where a namesake can’t be ruled out, the records are marked “To confirm” and kept apart.

  4. No guessing characters from pinyin

    It never infers a Chinese name from pinyin. Without character-by-character evidence, it keeps the name as found and says what evidence is still missing.

  5. From one person to a circle of people

    Once an identity is confirmed, it follows co-authored papers, shared patents, the same team and the same projects to find related candidates and possible ways in.

  6. Every check on record, ready to reuse

    Each identity check records which anchors and sources were used and what is still to confirm. When the same person turns up again, there’s no need to start from scratch.

Where it stops

HunterBuddy never invents identities, and never writes “possibly” as “confirmed”. It shows how strong the evidence is, and you decide whether it’s good enough to act on.
