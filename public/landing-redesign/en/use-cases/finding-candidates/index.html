Multi-source sourcing:
scattered leads, gathered into one list

Multi-source sourcing is a capability that searches the public web, papers and patents, open-source footprints and your authorized talent pool together. Records of the same person from different sources are checked first, then merged, then ranked.

The scarcest technical talent isn’t active on recruiting platforms, and doesn’t describe itself with standard job titles. HunterBuddy gathers scattered leads into one list you can review and keep using.

Boseek·Updated 2026-11-21

01

Why searching CVs alone misses the people who matter

  • The core people aren’t in CV databases

    The people active on platforms are mostly job seekers. Truly scarce technical talent leaves traces only in papers, patents, GitHub, project pages or company team pages.

  • Deep mapping work, invisible value

    Senior searches often start with talent pipelining and organizational research, long before any offer. If the process leaves no record, the client sees only “no interviews yet”.

  • No outright lies, but inflated contributions

    Teamwork becomes “led by me”, taking part becomes “owned”, and a peripheral module becomes a core one. CVs look more complete than ever, and judging them gets harder.

  • Technical depth is hard to verify

    Anyone can write “familiar with large models, robotics or chips”. Few have built the core research or key modules, and without evidence, recruiters get led by job titles.

  • The more senior, the less patience for an unprepared approach

    Senior technical circles are fairly closed and especially wary of templated outreach. If you don’t know what someone has been working on lately, a first conversation rarely lands.

  • Rival teams have moved on, your information hasn’t

    Expansion, layoffs and changes of key leaders usually show up first in papers, careers pages and reorganizations. Nobody can track it all by hand, and by the time the news reaches you, the best candidates may already be gone.

02

How HunterBuddy finds people

  1. From one platform to many sources

    HunterBuddy doesn’t stop at the platforms. It gathers leads from the public web, papers, patents, GitHub and the talent pool you’ve authorized it to import, and it shows which sources have been covered and which are still blank.

  2. Mapping work kept as an asset you can review

    Every search keeps each candidate’s sources, match reasons, evidence links, current employment status and open questions. You deliver more than a list: you deliver a trail of evidence you can walk the client through.

  3. Cross-checked, not taken on the CV’s word

    It compares CVs, public profiles, papers, patents and career history side by side, and merges records of the same person. Anything without a reliable source is marked “To confirm”, never written up as a conclusion.

  4. Real technical depth, beyond titles and keywords

    It gathers a candidate’s key papers, open-source projects, research areas and collaborators, to show whether their work sits closer to core research, key modules or engineering integration. It doesn’t make the technical call for you. It puts the evidence side by side.

  5. People, teams and relationships filled in before you reach out

    For each candidate it reconstructs, as far as it can, their current organization, role, team, collaboration network and public career history. You open with what they’ve been working on lately, not a templated script.

  6. A target company’s talent structure, kept as an updatable baseline

    From public job postings, paper authorship and people’s seniority, it lays out a target company’s core members and likely teams, as a baseline to compare against next time.

Where it stops

HunterBuddy doesn’t send mass messages or give one-click verdicts. It automates searching, enriching, checking, merging and ranking, and leaves judging people, conversations and decisions to you.
