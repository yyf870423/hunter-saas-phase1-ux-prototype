The usual approach

How to build a talent map

Talent mapping is a core recruiting skill. You do it when a mandate comes in, and you do it to get to know a sector. It usually takes these steps:

  1. Set the scope

    Choose the target and peer companies, and the functions and levels to cover. Make the scope too wide and the map is never finished.

  2. Build the structure

    Piece together teams and reporting lines from company websites, team pages, press releases and careers pages.

  3. Fill in the names

    Who holds each role, how long they have been in it and where they were before. Public profiles, paper authors and patent inventors are all leads.

  4. Verify

    The same person may appear under different names and titles. Confirm that it is the same person, and that they are still there.

  5. Mark the gaps

    Where you know a role exists but not who holds it, flag it so you can fill it in later.

Where it gets hard

The hard part isn’t finding information. It’s fitting it together.

  • Information is scattered

    Team pages, press releases, papers, careers pages and social platforms each hold only a small piece.

  • Same names, different titles

    Different people share a name, and one person is written differently in different places. Merge the wrong records and the whole map goes wrong with them.

  • Maps go stale

    Reorganizations and people moving on mean a map from six months ago may already be out of date.

  • Disconnected from your database

    Whether someone on the map is already a candidate in your database often has to be checked one by one.

FAQ

What people ask
about talent mapping.

What goes into a talent map?

The org structure and reporting lines of the target companies and teams, who holds each role, how long they have been in it and where they were before, plus the source of each detail and where the gaps are.

I already have talent maps in Excel. Can I keep using them?

Yes. Import talent maps from Excel or similar formats and keep building on them. Imported records go through the same identity checks and de-duplication.

How often should a talent map be updated?

There’s no fixed rule. Reorganizations and people moving on both make a map go stale. You can have HunterBuddy check for you regularly, such as changes to a target company’s careers page or updates to the public profiles of people you follow, and it alerts you when something is worth a look.

Can people found in papers and patents go straight onto the map?

They can go on as leads, but their identity needs checking first. Co-authoring a paper doesn’t mean a reporting line. HunterBuddy marks what is still unverified.
