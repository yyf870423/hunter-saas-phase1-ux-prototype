The usual approach

How candidate nurturing usually works

Candidate nurturing is a long game. Someone who isn’t looking today may be your next placement six months from now. It usually involves these steps:

  1. Pick your priority candidates

    Not everyone needs regular contact. Start with the people you value most and are most likely to work with.

  2. Watch for changes

    A reorganization, a new manager, several years in the same role, an updated public profile: any of these can be the moment to talk.

  3. Check your live searches

    When something changes, see whether you have a suitable role, or whether a new opening has come up in your network.

  4. Understand their motivation

    A team change doesn’t mean someone wants to leave. Find out what really matters to them this time.

  5. Keep the record current

    After the conversation, note what has changed so it’s there next time.

Where it gets hard

The hard part isn’t staying in touch. It’s knowing when to get in touch.

  • Too many people to follow

    Your database keeps growing, and so does your list of priority candidates. Keeping an eye on each one is hard.

  • Changes go unnoticed

    Signals like team changes and time in role are scattered across public sources. Unless you go looking, you won’t know.

  • Roles and people don’t connect

    You have a role on your desk but can’t think who in your database would fit.

  • Records go stale

    A CV from two years ago may be a long way from where the candidate is now.

FAQ

What people ask
about candidate nurturing.

How often should I keep in touch with candidates?

There’s no fixed rule. Showing up at the right moment matters more than regular check-ins. A team change, a new manager or several years in the same role are often good reasons to talk, and HunterBuddy alerts you when it spots changes like these.

How does it know a candidate’s situation has changed?

It looks at public information, such as profile updates, reorganizations at the candidate’s company and changes to its careers page. You can have it check regularly. It only alerts you to changes worth your time, and stays quiet when there’s nothing new.

Does a change mean they want to move?

No. A team change doesn’t mean someone wants to leave. HunterBuddy lays out the signals and the reasons. You need to ask whether they’re open to a move and what really drives them.

How do I update a candidate’s record after we talk?

Send HunterBuddy their new CV. Their profile and matches will update. You can also bring in your candidate database and talent maps, which go through the same identity checks and de-duplication.
