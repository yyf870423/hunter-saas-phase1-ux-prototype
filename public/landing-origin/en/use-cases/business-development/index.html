The usual approach

How business development usually works

Independent recruiters and senior search consultants rarely win new clients through cold calls. More often, they keep a close eye on a few familiar sectors, notice which companies are growing, and work their network to reach the decision-maker. Broadly, it comes down to five steps:

  1. Watch for signals

    Funding news, new roles on a careers page, leadership changes, new business lines and new plants can all mean a company is about to hire.

  2. Check the role is real

    A job posting is not a mandate. Find out whether the role is still open, whether the headcount is approved and why they are hiring now.

  3. Find the hiring manager

    Work out who leads the team, who makes the decision, and which part of the process HR owns.

  4. Find a way in

    Go direct, or ask for an introduction. Among your clients, the investors you know and your former colleagues, who knows the hiring manager well enough to put you in touch?

  5. Do your homework first

    Learn what the company does and who the team needs before you reach out. You’ll be taken more seriously than if you mass-mail CVs.

Where it gets hard

The hard part isn’t the call. It’s knowing who to call.

  • Signals are scattered

    Funding news is in the media, roles are on careers pages, people moves are on LinkedIn and Maimai. It’s hard for one person to check them all every day.

  • Details don’t match

    The same company goes by different names in different places, and the same person has different titles on different platforms. Matching them up takes a lot of time.

  • The moment is easy to miss

    By the time you notice a company is hiring, another recruiter may already be talking to them.

  • Research gets lost

    The companies and people you looked into last time are scattered across notes and spreadsheets, so next time you start from scratch.

FAQ

What people ask
about business development.

Where can recruiters find business development leads?

Funding news, company websites and careers pages, leadership and team changes, news of new business lines and plants, and your own clients and network. HunterBuddy puts them side by side and notes where each lead came from.

If a role is on the careers page, can I pitch for the mandate?

Not necessarily. A job posting is only a lead. First confirm whether the role is really open, whether the headcount is approved and who makes the decision. HunterBuddy lists what still needs confirming, and you confirm it by talking to people.

Will it contact clients for me?

No. It finds the leads, the hiring manager and the route in, with reasons and sources. Who to contact, when to reach out and how to pitch are up to you.

How often does it check my sectors?

You decide, for example once a week. It only alerts you to changes worth your time, merges duplicates and stays quiet when there’s nothing new.
