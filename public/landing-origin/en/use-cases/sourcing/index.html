The usual approach

How candidate sourcing usually works

When a new search comes in, experienced recruiters usually work like this:

  1. Understand the JD

    Talk it through with the hiring manager: which requirements are must-haves, which are nice-to-haves, and what the team is really missing.

  2. Pick target companies and teams

    Work out which companies and teams the right people are most likely to come from, and which companies are off-limits.

  3. Start with who you already have

    Go through your talent maps and CV database, and put past research to work first.

  4. Then look further afield

    Use the public web, careers pages, papers, patents and open-source communities to find the people your database is missing.

  5. Build the longlist

    Rank by fit, note why each person fits and what still needs confirming, then decide who to approach first.

Where it gets hard

The hard part isn’t finding people. It’s knowing they’re the right ones.

  • Your own resources sit unused

    The right person is in your CV database, but the search doesn’t surface them, or you’ve forgotten them.

  • CVs fall out of date

    A CV from two years ago may no longer reflect someone’s current role or projects.

  • The same person, several times

    Your database, recruiting platforms and papers each hold a version. Without merging them, you end up contacting the same person twice.

  • Looking good on paper isn’t enough

    Strong papers don’t prove mass-production or leadership experience.

FAQ

What people ask
about sourcing.

Where can recruiters source candidates from?

Your existing candidates and talent maps, the public web and careers pages, papers and patents, open-source projects, and the CVs and documents you upload. HunterBuddy notes where each lead came from, and marks which channels have been searched and which haven’t yet.

Will it contact candidates for me?

No. It gives you a longlist with the reasons and sources. Who to approach first, and how, is up to you.

How is it different from searching on a recruiting platform?

Recruiting platforms are where you search for and contact candidates, and HunterBuddy doesn’t replace them. It does the research before and during a search: putting your own resources to work first, then bringing public information together to check identities and cite sources. CVs downloaded from these platforms can be uploaded and checked too.

Can I take the match results at face value?

Treat them as sourced guidance, not a final verdict. Anything without a reliable source is marked as unconfirmed. Some things you only find out by talking to the person, such as how big a team they really lead or whether they’re open to a move. HunterBuddy leaves those explicitly to you.
